Why a brief like this is hard

  • Coverage versus throughput. A wall-plate AP in every guest room gives clean coverage but nothing to roam between; a ceiling AP per corridor is cheaper to run but collapses under dense evening traffic. The design mixes both, and the price follows the mix.
  • PoE budgeting: 383 access points plus cameras on the same switching layer means the power budget per switch, not per port, decides how many units you can hang on it.
  • A chassis core with dual management modules is where lead time comes from - and where substitution is least acceptable in a hotel that cannot go offline.
  • Video storage sizing is coupled to the camera package: retention days, bitrate and channel count had to be fixed before either package was bought.

How this brief runs through our process

  1. 01Split the requirement into three separately quotable sub-packages - wireless, switching, storage - because no single factory is competitive across all three.
  2. 02Quote the 214-unit wall-plate type and the 169-unit ceiling type separately; they are almost never made on the same line.
  3. 03Test one AP of each type under a real 60-client load and check the PoE budget on the intended switch, instead of accepting "up to 60 users" from a datasheet.
  4. 04Confirm the chassis configuration item by item - chassis, power, management, line cards - so the quote cannot come back as a stripped-down version.
  5. 05Check storage channel count and licence terms against the 219-camera package before ordering; licences are the hidden line in every storage quote.
