Video surveillance · 15-floor hotel

A 15-floor hotel, 219 cameras, 13 camera types

A hotel operator issued a floor-by-floor surveillance design: AI perimeter protection, face detection, multi-sensor and fisheye coverage of shared areas, thermal cameras on the perimeter, and elevator cars linked to access control. The job is not the camera count - it is keeping thirteen camera types, seventeen levels and one storage budget aligned.

Why a brief like this is hard

  • Thirteen camera types on one site. Each has its own sensor, lens and low-light behaviour, so a single "cameras" line item cannot be quoted - every type needs its own price, lead time and certificate.
  • Quantities follow the floor function: guest-room floors take fixed domes by the dozen, penthouses take motorised units, offices take fewer but higher-spec models, and the roof and rotating track need long-range and thermal units.
  • Elevator cars are an integration problem, not a camera problem - car camera, control module, host and transmission unit must match the lift controller.
  • Storage was budgeted against the whole site, so bitrate and retention had to be settled before the cameras were bought, not after.

How this brief runs through our process

  1. 01Rebuild the floor-by-floor tables into one RFQ per camera type, describing resolution, lens, IR distance and mounting the same way for every factory.
  2. 02Flag the types where an equivalent can be quoted without losing certification, and where it cannot - thermal and 3D units are the usual "no".
  3. 03Collect samples of the two highest-volume types - the 57-unit 2 MP dome and the 56-unit 4 MP bullet - and test them at night, since low-light performance is where cheap substitutes fall apart.
  4. 04Check that certificates and test reports name each model actually shipping, not the series.
  5. 05Inspect the highest-volume types against the approved sample before consolidation, and confirm the elevator package matches the lift controller before it leaves the factory.
