Why buyers come to us

Four things that go wrong when you buy direct

Each one is a process failure rather than a price problem - which is why it survives order after order.

01

The three quotes are not the same product

One factory quotes 8.8 property class, one quotes 4.8 from the same photo, one leaves the coating out. The prices look 30% apart and none of them is comparable. Normalising the RFQ is the first thing we do - and it is usually where the saving actually is.

02

The certificate names a different model

A CE or UL document arrives, looks right, and covers a model from two years ago rather than the one in your carton. In ELV, fire and electrical product this is the most common reason a container is refused at the border.

03

The sample came off a different line

Approved samples get made on the good line with a senior operator. Bulk comes off whichever line is free that week. Nobody compares the two unless someone is standing in the warehouse with the approved piece.

04

Four suppliers, four shipments, four document sets

Cameras from one belt, cable from another, tiles from a third. Shipping separately eats the discount you negotiated, and nobody checks the carton marks against the packing list.

Questions buyers ask first

Before you send a brief

How is this different from buying on a B2B marketplace?

On a marketplace the listing decides which factories you can see, and the number you get is the one that factory chose to give you. We work outward from the industrial belt: several plants in the county that actually makes the product, asked the same questions, with the answers normalised. You see the spread instead of one price.

How do you charge?

Either a fixed project fee (matching, audit, inspection) or a commission on order value for full agency work. Which model applies is stated in a written scope before we start, along with what you receive and how long it takes. Nothing is charged before you approve that scope.

Do we have to buy through you?

No. For matching and inspection we can work as your independent pair of hands on the ground and you contract the factory directly. For full agency work we manage the order and the follow-up, and that is written into the scope.

Can you handle several product families in one shipment?

Yes - that is the reason for working a belt map instead of a single supplier. Cameras, cable, tiles and hardware can leave from different provinces and still load into one container against a plan you approve.

What happens if the goods fail inspection?

We stop them, document the gap against the approved sample and put the fix to the factory. You decide whether to rework, replace or reject. We do not release goods you have not approved.

Why do you publish the belt data?

Because knowing the market is the difference between an agent and a mailbox. It also lets you check us: every figure on a belt profile links to the government or association source it came from.
